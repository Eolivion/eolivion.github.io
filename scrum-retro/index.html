Scrum Retro for Jira

Getting started

Scrum Retro runs your sprint retrospective inside Jira, and the action items become Jira issues. It runs inside Jira and makes no calls to outside servers.

Open it

Open the project and pick Scrum Retro in the project's navigation (it may be under More). Anyone who opens it in that project can join a retro.

Start a retro

  • Sprint: pick the board and the sprint the retro is about. The active sprint, or the last closed one, comes first.
  • Format: Went well / To improve / Action ideas, Start Stop Continue, Mad Sad Glad, 4Ls, Drop Add Keep Improve, Sailboat, Starfish, KALM, Hopes and Fears, or your own columns.
  • Anonymous cards: nobody sees who wrote what.
  • Votes per person, and whether someone can put more than one vote on the same card.

The steps

  1. Write. Everyone adds cards at the same time. You only see your own cards, so nobody follows the first idea on the board.
  2. Group. All cards show up. Drag a card onto a similar one, or use Group with…, to make one topic out of them.
  3. Vote. Everyone spends their votes on the topics they want to talk about. Totals stay hidden until the discussion.
  4. Discuss. Topics come in vote order. The facilitator picks the one on screen; add action items as you go.
  5. Wrap up. Turn each action item into a Jira issue with one click (it gets the label retro-action), and share the summary.

The person who starts the retro is the facilitator and moves it from step to step. Anyone can choose Run this retro to take over. There's a timer for each step.

Between retros

  • Last retro's actions: open action items from the previous retro in the same project come back, so you can mark them done or carry them over.
  • Sprint numbers: how many issues and story points the sprint finished, and which issues are carrying over.
  • Team mood: an anonymous 1 to 5 check. Only the average is shown, after the writing step.
  • Summary: a ready-to-paste summary (Markdown, for Confluence, Slack or an email) and a CSV.

Permissions and data

The app reads Jira with the permissions of the person using it, and creates issues as that person. Retros, cards and votes are kept in Atlassian's own storage for the app; see the privacy policy for exactly what's kept and for how long.

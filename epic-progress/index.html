Epic Progress & Hierarchy Tree for Jira Dashboards

Getting started

Epic Progress & Hierarchy Tree is a widget for the new Jira dashboards. It shows your epics, their children and subtasks as a tree you can open right on the dashboard, with a progress bar and totals at every level. It runs inside Jira and makes no calls to outside servers.

Add the widget

  1. Open a dashboard and choose Edit, then Add widget.
  2. Search for Epic Progress and add Epic Progress & Hierarchy Tree.
  3. Fill in its settings, check the preview beside them and choose Add.

The widget is available on the new Jira dashboards. It isn't offered as a gadget on classic dashboards.

Choose what goes at the top

Top level (JQL) picks the work items at the top of the tree. The default is every epic that isn't done. Use any field to narrow it, like project = SHOP AND issuetype = Epic AND fixVersion = "Q4". Their children come along on their own, so you don't need a second query for them.

Levels sets how far down the tree goes: two levels, like epics and their stories; three, down to subtasks; or four, when your site has a level above epics.

Add up a field

In Add up, choose story points, original estimate, time spent or any other number field. Each level then shows what its children add up to.

  • A value set on the parent itself shows in brackets beside the total, like 26 (8), so it isn't counted twice.
  • When only some children have a value, the widget says so beside the total, like 4 of 5 estimated, so you know what it leaves out.
  • Progress by fills the bars by work items done or by the field done.

Open the tree

Click the arrow beside a work item to open its children right there, or choose Expand all. Click a key to open the work item in Jira. Each work item shows once, under its parent.

The top level shows up to 200 work items. On very large trees, some children load when you open their parent, and the totals grow as they do.

Dashboard filters

When Use this dashboard's filters on the top level is on, the dashboard's filter bar narrows the top level: space, status, priority, work type and dates. When a filter can't be applied, the widget names it, so nothing is left out quietly.

Who sees what

The widget reads work items as the person looking at the dashboard. Everyone sees only the work items they can already open in Jira, and the totals count only those.

Privacy Policy

Last updated: November 22, 2026

This policy covers the apps Eolivion builds for Atlassian Cloud: Scrum Poker by Eolivion (and its free edition, Scrum Poker Free by Eolivion), Scrum Retro by Eolivion (and its free edition, Scrum Retro Free by Eolivion), Issue and Work Item Templates by Eolivion, Time in Status, Cycle Time and Lead Time by Eolivion, Epic Progress & Hierarchy Tree for Jira Dashboards and Velocity Chart & Sprint Report for Jira Dashboards. Each app has its own section below, because each one keeps different things.

The short version

The apps read your Jira data inside Atlassian's own infrastructure, with your own permissions. Eolivion doesn't receive, store or sell any of it. There are no Eolivion servers involved.

Where the apps run

Every app is built on Atlassian Forge and runs entirely on Atlassian's platform. They make no requests to any server outside Atlassian. Your data stays where your Jira data already is, under Atlassian's hosting, data residency and security commitments. When an app asks Jira for data, it uses the permissions of the person using it, so it can't see anything that person can't already see.

Scrum Poker by Eolivion

Scrum Poker runs entirely on Atlassian's platform too, makes no requests outside Atlassian, and Eolivion can't see its data. It keeps some data so a session works for everyone in it.

Issue and Work Item Templates by Eolivion

Issue and Work Item Templates runs entirely on Atlassian's platform too, makes no requests outside Atlassian, and Eolivion can't see its data. It keeps the templates, the schedules and the result of each time a template is applied.

All of this lives in Atlassian Forge storage, on Atlassian's infrastructure. The work items, subtasks and links a template creates are regular Jira data and stay in Jira.

When a template is applied, the app creates and edits the work items itself and sets the person who created the work item as the reporter of the new items. Before applying from the work item's menu, it checks that the person can edit that work item and create work items in the project.

Once a week the app asks Atlassian which of the account IDs it holds belong to closed accounts, and removes them from the templates and schedules. A schedule whose reporter closed their account pauses.

Time in Status, Cycle Time and Lead Time by Eolivion

Time in Status runs entirely on Atlassian's platform too, makes no requests outside Atlassian, and Eolivion can't see its data. It reads work items and their status history when someone runs a report, with that person's own Jira permissions, and doesn't keep the results.

Epic Progress & Hierarchy Tree for Jira Dashboards

Epic Progress runs entirely on Atlassian's platform, makes no requests outside Atlassian, and Eolivion can't see its data. It reads work items and their fields when someone opens a dashboard with the widget, with that person's own Jira permissions, and doesn't keep the results. It stores nothing of its own: the widget's settings (title, JQL, levels and the field it adds up) are saved by Jira with the dashboard.

Velocity Chart & Sprint Report for Jira Dashboards

Velocity Chart runs entirely on Atlassian's platform, makes no requests outside Atlassian, and Eolivion can't see its data. It reads a board, its sprints and their sprint reports when someone opens a dashboard with the widget, with that person's own Jira permissions, and doesn't keep the results. It stores nothing of its own: the widget's settings (title, board and how many sprints) are saved by Jira with the dashboard.

All of this lives in Atlassian Forge storage, on Atlassian's infrastructure. None of it identifies a person. The report results, including work item keys, summaries and assignee names, are calculated in the browser of the person running the report and aren't stored anywhere. The dashboard gadget and widget keep their last summary in that person's browser for up to an hour: counts and times and, in the view of open work items by age, the key, summary and status of up to 10 work items.

What Eolivion receives

When the app is installed, Atlassian shares licensing and contact details about the customer who installs the app (for example, the site name and a billing or technical contact). Eolivion uses these only to manage the license and to answer support requests.

If you contact support, Eolivion keeps your message and contact details for as long as it takes to resolve the request, and for up to 12 months afterwards for reference. You can ask for them to be deleted at any time.

Sharing

Eolivion doesn't sell or share personal data. The only third party involved is Atlassian, as the host of the app and the Marketplace.

Your rights

You can ask what Eolivion holds about you, ask for a correction or ask for deletion, under the GDPR, the Brazilian LGPD or any similar law that applies to you. Open a request on the support portal. Eolivion will answer within 30 days.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version will be published here with a new date. Material changes will be announced on the app's Marketplace listing.
